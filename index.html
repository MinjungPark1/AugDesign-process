03 · SERVICE SELECTION

3 Candidates before deciding the services…

I compared three service directions against the same business and UX questions before choosing the group opportunity for Uber.

Evaluation question1. Google Maps: Free restroom finder2. BrickLink: Outside-purchase tracking3. Uber: Shared costs with friends *
Who needs it most?Tourists exploring unfamiliar cities, delivery workers, parents, and people with specific restroom or accessibility needs.Custom LEGO builders buying parts across multiple stores.Groups of people repeatedly sharing rides, especially when participants change.
Would current users pay extra?Likely low willingness. Keep essential information free: public access, cost, opening hours, and entrance directions.Most plausible direct-payment opportunity: frequent builders can measure time saved and duplicate purchases avoided.Likely limited willingness for a separate subscription. A small transaction fee or membership benefit could be tested.
How could the company benefit?More frequent Maps use during outings and stronger trust in its local information.More completed builds and repeat purchases of remaining parts.More completed group bookings and repeat rides.
What would encourage repeat use?Needing a restroom in another neighborhood or city, with reliable information available each time.Starting another build or receiving another outside order.Planning another outing with the same friend group.
Main commercial uncertainty?Keeping availability and entry rules accurate costs money. Many potential users already use Maps, so the benefit may primarily be retention.The feature may be too narrow for a subscription, and importing purchases could help users spend elsewhere.Invitees may already use Uber. Sharing a ride can also replace several separate bookings.
Best early success measure?Percentage of searches that lead to successful use of a free restroom, plus repeat feature use.Time to reconcile an order, plus repeat purchasing.Invite-to-first-booking conversion and repeat group bookings.
Strongest business argument?Make Maps more useful throughout a day in an unfamiliar city.Save committed hobbyists measurable time and money.Make organizing and paying for recurring group rides easier.
04 · CUSTOMER JOURNEY

Customer journey with need statement: As-is // To-be

Need statement. When a friend organizes a group outing, they need one place to coordinate the plan and each person’s commitment, so booking does not become a second job.

STEP 01

Plan the outing

Friends agree on what they want to do.

AS-IS · COORDINATE IN CHAT

The organizer asks who is coming, compares prices, and collects answers across messages.

Friction: the plan is scattered.
TO-BE · START A GROUP

The organizer names the occasion and invites followed friends from the Uber flow.

Gain: one shared starting point.
STEP 02

Book the ride

Someone chooses a route and vehicle.

AS-IS · ONE PERSON DECIDES

The lead picks a car before everyone has seen the same option or agreed to the cost.

Friction: responsibility stays with the lead.
TO-BE · CHOOSE TOGETHER

The group sees the route, car, and estimated allocation before the request is sent.

Gain: the choice is legible to everyone.
STEP 03

Split during the ride

Payment becomes part of the experience.

AS-IS · SPLIT AFTER MATCHING

The group may rely on an equal split later or settle a custom amount outside the main flow.

Friction: the amount arrives late.
TO-BE · SHOW EXACT SHARES

Each invited rider sees the amount they would authorize, down to the cent.

Gain: no surprise allocation.
STEP 04

Take the ride

The group moves from planning to doing.

AS-IS · WAIT FOR UPDATES

The lead carries the status of the booking and has to relay changes to everyone else.

Friction: one person becomes support.
TO-BE · GROUP STATUS

Everyone can see the ride status and the group chat in the same shared context.

Gain: fewer coordination messages.
STEP 05

Review and settle

The experience closes without guesswork.

AS-IS · RECONCILE LATER

The lead may remind friends, calculate a custom share, or wait for reimbursement.

Friction: the task continues after the trip.
TO-BE · AUTHORIZE YOUR OWN SHARE

Each rider approves their own amount before the organizer requests the vehicle.

Gain: less work for the friend who books.
STEP 06

Close the occasion

A good group moment can happen again.

AS-IS · END WITH A RECEIPT

The service records the trip, but not necessarily the group relationship around it.

Friction: no obvious next occasion.
TO-BE · KEEP THE GROUP VISIBLE

The Account hub keeps active and past groups available for another shared plan.

Gain: a reason to return together.

06 · REALITY CHECK

What was actually hard about vibecoding

Fast generation made the first draft easy. The difficult part was deciding what to keep, what to correct, and what the prototype needed to prove.

CHALLENGE 01

“Sometimes takes a lot of time, but I don’t know what’s going on.”

When the system changes several things at once, it becomes hard to identify the decision behind each change.

PRACTICE 02

“Easy to go with top-down, because it’s easy to build…”

Starting broad helps create momentum, but the important constraints still need to be added deliberately.

PRACTICE 03

“Giving great examples is the way I can communicate easily.”

A concrete screen, state, or sentence often communicates the intended direction better than an abstract request.
