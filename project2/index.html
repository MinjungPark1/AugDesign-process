A BROADER GAP TO INVESTIGATE

One person’s working life.
Several employers’ systems.

Can a worker-owned view connect applications, changing shifts and pay records across unrelated employers—even when they use different tools? Existing features make this a usability and interoperability question to test.

Screening is still an employer-policy problem

Indeed moves candidates who fail required screener questions to the employer’s Rejected list; employers can review and restore them. Investigate whether prior experience is unnecessarily required for teachable work. The design still needs an employer-facing solution.

EXPANDED CONCEPT TO EXPLORE

A first-job companion
that grows with your work.

Start with first-time hourly applicants. Continue supporting them when they take a second job or juggle shifts at several workplaces.

The original T1 application idea now draws on the shortlist’s W6 idea: shift coordination for workers with two jobs. The #8 ranking and $149 price describe the original application concept; the expanded service needs fresh validation.

WHO BENEFITS—AND WHO PAYS?
Workers
Less schedule juggling, clearer commitments, and a record of expected and received pay.
Employers
Potentially fewer avoidable clashes and clearer availability. Test whether those benefits justify paying.
Business model
Employer-funded hiring remains the starting hypothesis. Do employers value the ongoing companion, or should another funding model support it?
Worker control
Share selected availability with an employer, while keeping other job names, wages and personal commitments private by default.
What to learn from worker interviews
  1. Show me how you combined last week’s schedules. Where did you record changes?
  2. Tell me about a shift clash or a last-minute change. Who did you contact, and how?
  3. How do you check hours, pay periods, payslips and payments from each job?
  4. What work information would you share with a manager—and what would you keep private?
  5. Would you maintain a combined record manually if the systems could not connect?
The opportunity continues after “You’re hired”: one worker, many commitments, a clearer view.

DEFINE 06 / DESIGN CHALLENGE
FROM BROAD OPPORTUNITY TO A FOCUSED V1

The exploration began with first-job applications, then expanded to coordinating work and pay. The PRD focuses the prototype on one complete journey: resolving a conflict between two jobs.

HOW MIGHT WE…

Help hourly workers coordinate two jobs and confirm shift coverage without exposing their other employer’s schedule?

01 / SEE THE PROBLEM

Catch conflicts early.

Make overlapping shifts and insufficient travel time understandable across separate schedules.

02 / PROTECT THE PERSON

Keep private details private.

Give the worker a combined view. Show managers and eligible coworkers only the workplace shift being offered.

03 / CLOSE THE LOOP

Make responsibility clear.

Distinguish requesting help, a coworker volunteering, and a manager confirming the replacement.

WHO WE ARE DESIGNING FOR

Worker: an hourly restaurant worker with two jobs.
Coworker: a qualified teammate at the same workplace.
Manager: the person responsible for coverage and approval.

V1 FOCUS

Private schedule coordination and full-shift coverage within one participating workplace. The second employer does not need to join.

Hiring and pay tracking remain part of the broader exploration; they are outside this prototype. Employer-system connections and real notifications are also outside v1.

What would count as a successful first test?
4 of 5 workers

Detect and explain the seeded conflict without prompting.

4 of 5 workers

Create a valid coverage request without assistance.

Every participant

Correctly identify who is assigned and what the manager can see.

Proposed usability targets from the PRD—not achieved results. First validate recurring need, privacy expectations, manager participation and the burden of manual entry. An interface cannot create available staff.

Research notes

The 30 concepts and their scores come from the project’s discovery materials dated 2 October 2026. Ranking reflects exploratory judgment; prices are test assumptions.

  • Apple: iPod launch, 2001

    Supports portability and ease of use, not the gender-targeting anecdote.

  • Kakao: stories about KakaoTalk

    Company history discusses WhatsApp preceding KakaoTalk. Local adaptation is a design interpretation.

  • National Restaurant Association: State Statistics

    1M+ outlets is broad industry context, not this service’s addressable employer count.

  • Workday: Talent Acquisition
  • Fountain: About Us
  • Indeed: screener questions

    Required versus preferred qualifications and employer review of rejected applicants.

  • Homebase: how scheduling, time tracking and payroll connect
  • When I Work: multiple workplaces

    Documents account switching when employers use the same platform. It does not establish compatibility with every other scheduling system.

  • 7shifts: tips overview

    Distinguishes earned tips, tips paid on shift and amounts owed.
